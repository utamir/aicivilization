## Claim
There is no defensible single empirical AGI calendar curve for this simulator. The International AI Safety Report 2026 treats progress through 2030 as deeply uncertain, with plausible regimes from plateau/slowdown through continuation to rapid acceleration. Benchmark performance also has an evaluation gap with real-world capability.

METR's task-completion time horizon is useful inside its evaluation domain, but the current task suite is primarily software engineering, machine learning and cybersecurity. METR explicitly warns that measurements above roughly 16 hours are unreliable with the current suite.

## What it constrains in the model
- Never translate a METR-like benchmark trend into 1,000-hour, multi-month or multi-year autonomous-work claims.
- Cap the evidence-labelled horizon display at the measured reliability range.
- Use a separate, explicitly speculative dimensionless autonomy/capability state for deep-future mechanisms.
- Treat AGI and ASI as model-defined regimes requiring multiple conditions; do not claim their simulated date is an empirical forecast.
- Preserve multiple AI-progress scenarios and widen structural uncertainty with horizon.

## How we would know this note needs revision
Revisit when long-horizon task suites obtain reliable external validity far beyond the current range, when measurement methodology changes materially, or when strong real-world evidence resolves one or more of the current AI trajectory regimes.
