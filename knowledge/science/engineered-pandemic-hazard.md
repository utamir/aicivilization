## What the evidence actually says

The most useful recent elicitation is narrower than earlier versions of this note implied. FRI recruited 46 biology/biosecurity experts and 22 superforecasters. The median expert estimate was a **0.3% annual baseline risk of a human-caused epidemic causing 100,000 deaths**. Under a specified hypothetical LLM capability condition, the median expert estimate rose to **1.5%**; with LLM safeguards and mandatory nucleic-acid screening it fell near baseline, to **0.4%**.

Those numbers do **not** estimate a civilizational pandemic, extinction risk, or a universal “few percent per decade” catastrophic hazard. They are conditional forecasts around a defined epidemic endpoint and uncertain capability assumptions.

## What it constrains in the model

The simulator keeps a separate engineered-pathogen hazard because increased biotech capability can plausibly raise both defensive capability and misuse capability. Its numerical mapping is an explicit **stress-test assumption**: risk rises with capability/exposure and falls strongly with biosecurity/screening/response capacity.

Do not present the resulting simulated event frequency as empirically measured. Scenario differences are more defensible than the absolute number.

## Release validation question

AI Safety First should materially suppress engineered events relative to otherwise similar weak-biosecurity scenarios. If strong safeguards do not reduce simulated hazard, the mechanism is wrong. If the model routinely creates civilization-ending engineered pandemics from ordinary baseline settings, the calibration is also wrong.
